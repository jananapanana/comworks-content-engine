## What the old posts have in common

- **Occasion:** every post marks an event (year end, project end, funding). None starts from a problem the reader has
- **Audience:** the people in the post. Clients, teammates, friends. Comments come from them, almost none from strangers who recognised their own situation
- **Content:** scope lists ("performance marketing, organic social, rebranding, media buying"), gratitude, mood. No number from the work, no finding, no decision, no opinion
- **Surface:** emojis as bullets, Unicode bold headers, hashtags, "incredible journey", "hit different", "dope", "super brains", exclamation marks, a PS
- **Signal to a buyer:** Jana is busy and liked. Nothing about what she found out or how she thinks

## What the comments say

The warmest reactions came from people who worked with her. O2 got the most comments of the three, and they are about the avatar video. The content that would make a CEO or CMO stop (what the growth sprint changed, which channel converted, what the CRM foundation enables) is announced as headings and never shown.

## What the engine keeps from them

- English as the working language on LinkedIn
- First person, warm, direct address
- Jana names the people she worked with when they are public figures of the project (keep for released clients only)
- A visual with every post

## What the engine drops

Occasion-driven posting, scope lists, emoji bullets, Unicode bold, hashtags, superlatives, the PS line, job ads inside content posts.
