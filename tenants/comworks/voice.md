Jana's authentic register is documented in the jana-voice skill: substance first, no Floskeln, specific, direct disagreement, hands the decision to the reader, stops when the point is made. The LinkedIn voice is that register in English, written for a stranger who has the same problem.

## Guiding principle (Jana, 06.10.2026)

Relevant content, direct and never blurry, so the reader recognises their own situation in it. Every post names a concrete situation the reader has been in. Specific beats safe. A tool name, a number or a question is welcome when it makes the reader think "that is my case".

## Profile

- **Speaker:** first person singular. Jana speaks, never "we at COM works"
- **Address:** plain "you". The reader is a peer
- **Register:** operator who has done the work and reports what happened. Dry, specific, unhurried
- **Humour:** dry, rare, never a punchline
- **Sentences:** short. One point per sentence. Periods and colons, never dashes
- **Specifics over adjectives:** numbers, counts, dates, concrete objects ("the course dates sat inside the product title")
- **Structure of a post:** a specific situation, what was found, what was built or decided, one line that generalises. The general line comes last and is short
- **Endings:** a plain instruction, the claim itself, or one real question the reader can answer from their own work ("Which system owns that field in your stack?"). No "agree?", no call to book a call
- **Formatting:** no emojis, no hashtags, no bold. Paragraphs of one to three sentences, blank line between them. Numbered lists when the post is an evaluation or a method (see admired posts A1, A2), without trailing periods
- **Vocabulary she uses:** system, handover, enable, signal, filter, cadence, score, build, runs without me
- **Banned words:** game-changer, revolutionary, holistic, synergies, leverage, unlock, delve, journey, empower, seamless, cutting-edge, supercharge
- **Banned patterns:** "not X, but Y" reveals, "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", lists of three adjectives, any sentence that praises COM works, vague claims without a situation behind them
- **Tool names:** allowed in hook and copy when the tool is part of the reader's situation ("Your HubSpot score is all firmographics"). Not allowed as the headline promise of COM works (no "HubSpot expert" framing). Decision Jana 06.10.2026
- **Questions:** allowed as hook or close when they name a situation the reader is in ("Can your sales team say which 20 percent of the CRM matter?"). Not allowed as engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?"). Decision Jana 06.10.2026

## From the old posts to the new ones

| Old (2025) | New |
| --- | --- |
| Starts from an occasion: year end, project end, funding | Starts from a situation the reader has on a Tuesday |
| Lists the scope: "performance marketing, organic social, rebranding" | Shows one finding from the work, with the number |
| Thanks and names | One decision, one result, one takeaway |
| Emojis as bullets, Unicode bold, hashtags, "incredible journey", a PS | Plain text, numbered list when it is a method or an evaluation, no decoration |
| Credibility buried ("reminded me of the hyper-growth days at Trade Republic and Kry") | Credibility in the first two lines when it is the point |
| Comments from friends and clients | Comments from people who recognised their own case |

## What a hook looks like

The hook is the first sentence. It states a concrete, slightly surprising fact from the work. Examples from the samples:

- "We looked at the 50 highest-revenue accounts of a B2B platform. Industry, headcount, founding year, country: no pattern."
- "The course dates were inside the product title. Three formats, no rule."
- "We handed a monthly newsletter to an AI system in June. Here is what the first issue did."

A question works as a hook when the reader can answer it from their own work: "Does your lead score have more demographic than behavioural dimensions?" Not a hook: "Most companies get lead scoring wrong." (opinion without a situation) or "Want to know how we doubled open rates?" (bait, sales).
